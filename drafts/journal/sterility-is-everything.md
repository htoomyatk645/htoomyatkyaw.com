---
title: Sterility Is Everything
date: 2025-06-09
category: medicine
summary: My first weekend shift as the youngest anesthesia tech in the ORs, a pulse oximeter, and the glare from a surgical tech that taught me what a sterile field really means.
---
I started in the ORs at seventeen, the youngest anesthesia technician there. It was my first weekend shift, and my trainer and I were asked to bring a pulse oximeter into an OR. We masked up, he handed me the device, and I walked in with him close behind me.

The anesthesia attending was on the far side of the drapes. My job sounded simple: get the device to them without disturbing the sterile field. The trouble was that I didn't really understand what that meant yet.

I moved as carefully as I could. Then I caught a look from one of the surgical technologists, the kind of look you don't need explained. I was an arm's length from the sterile field. My trainer quietly told me from behind that one more step and the team would have had to restart the case.

I still think about that moment. Until then I hadn't seen how much quiet work goes into keeping a patient safe during a procedure, or how much of it rests on every single person in the room, including the new kid holding a pulse ox.

Afterwards my trainer walked me through the etiquette of the OR, and I've kept every word of it. Never pick up something that was dropped on purpose. Always know where the sterile field is. And the rule I still repeat to myself: if it's blue and clear, don't touch it.

The same goes for helping providers gown and glove. Once they've scrubbed in they're sterile, and it's on us to keep them that way. One of them put it best, laughing as they said it: "Don't touch me, I'm sterile."

That was my first real lesson in the OR. Sterility is everything.
