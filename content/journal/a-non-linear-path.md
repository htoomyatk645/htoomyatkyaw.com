My path so far doesn't run in a straight line. I interned at the New York State Senate, worked as an anesthesia technician at Albany Medical Center, did computational biology research at the RNA Institute, and then co-founded a podcast app while starting at Tufts.

It can look unfocused from the outside. I think each stop taught me something that I use every day.

## The Senate taught me deadlines

At the State Senate I designed print and digital materials that went out to constituents across the state. Legislative deadlines don't move, and several people usually needed something at once. I learned to finish the work, and to let "perfect" go when the calendar said so.

## The operating room taught me calm

As an anesthesia technician I prepared and maintained operating rooms and fixed equipment problems before procedures began. In that setting people use closed-loop communication: you repeat an instruction back so that nobody has to guess whether it was heard.

I still work that way. In a three-person startup a dropped message can cost a week.

## The lab taught me patience

At the RNA Institute I worked with real RNA datasets in R and on the Linux command line, studying a neurodegenerative disorder called spinocerebellar ataxia. Research moves slowly, and most of what you try doesn't work. That turned out to be good preparation for building a product.

## What ties it together

Outside of work and class I train Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, cook, and practice languages. Jiu-Jitsu keeps teaching me the same lesson the rest of this path did: you improve by showing up and being a beginner again, over and over.

The line through all of it is curiosity about how people think, and a wish to build things that are good for them.
