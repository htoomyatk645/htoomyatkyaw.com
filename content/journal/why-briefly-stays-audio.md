When people hear that Briefly makes podcasts shorter, they usually assume it writes summaries. But it doesn't. Briefly finds the 5-15 minute briefs from each episode that are worth hearing and plays them to you in the host's own voice.

This deliberate choice came early on, because we value the human connection that podcasts bring, something our competitors can't say.

## A summary tastes different

A written or AI-voiced summary tells you a compressed version of what someone said. A brief lets you hear them say it in the way the speaker intended. The pause before an answer, the laugh, and the way a guest slows down when a topic matters to them are all part of what makes a worthwhile conversation, and none of it survives the filter in summarized form.

There is also a question of trust. A summary asks you to believe that the tool understood the speaker correctly. With a brief there is nothing to take on faith, because you are listening to the original. If you want the full context, the whole episode is one tap away. Not only that, we offer built-in tools to help you understand and explore.

> Briefly lets you stay in control.

## It fits when people actually listen

People listen to podcasts while they commute, cook, or drive. Their hands and eyes are busy. A summary you have to read asks for the one kind of attention they don't have in that moment.

Staying in audio means Briefly works in the places podcasts already live. It is also why CarPlay support mattered to us for launch, and why the touch targets in the app are large.

## What it cost us

Finding briefs is harder than summarizing. The system has to find a moment that makes sense on its own, context-free, brief it cleanly at the start and the end, and do that across thousands of shows. We built the pipeline so that all of the expensive work happens once, when an episode comes in. Nothing heavy runs while you scroll the feed, which is what keeps it fast.

I noticed that AI-voiced summaries are not only computationally expensive. As a listener, I get annoyed listening to a semi-robotic voice. It felt creepy, which is why the early pivot was a must.

## What I'd tell another founder

Pick the one thing your product will not do, and say it out loud early. For us it was "we will not change what people said." A line like that settles a lot of design debates before they start.
