Before Briefly launched, we had a waitlist and no app in the store. Our job over the summer was to build an audience that already trusted us by the time the product arrived. We did it with one format: a seven-slide Instagram carousel, posted twice a day.

By the end of the summer the account had reached about 1.5 million views and grown to roughly 2,700 followers, all of it organic. Here is what I took from it.

## One format beats ten ideas

We chose a single repeatable unit and gave every slide one job. Slide one hooks. The slides in the middle carry the evidence. One slide is built to be saved, and the last one asks for something.

Keeping the format fixed meant that each new post only changed the content. That made it much easier to tell what was working, because only one thing was different each time.

## Do it by hand first, then teach it

Two carousels a day is too much for three people to design by hand. But we still designed the first ones slide by slide, because that was the only way to learn the rules: how long a hook can be, where the evidence should sit, and which slide earns the save.

Once we knew the rules, we wrote them down as instructions an AI could follow: a copywriter, a layout generator, an exporter, and an illustration style guide. After that the AI could draft a complete carousel in our format and voice, and our time went into the ideas.

:::note The order matters
Hand first, then instructions. You can only teach taste that you have already worked out yourself.
:::

## Teach something real

Every carousel belonged to one of three pillars: memory science, podcast recommendations, and Briefly's own features. My favorite was memory science: why hearing an idea helps you keep it, and why short clips fit the way attention works.

That happens to be what I study. People can tell when an account cares about its subject.

## If you're starting from zero

Pick one format and one posting time, and keep both for a month before you judge anything. Consistency is what makes the rest possible.
