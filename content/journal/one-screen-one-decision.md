I study Cognitive and Brain Science alongside Computer Science, and I design a consumer app. The two overlap more than I expected. Most of my design rules are things I first met in a lecture about how the mind works.

## Attention is a limited resource

Nobody opens an app with their full attention. They are on a train, in a kitchen, or between two other things. I try to design for that person and not for an imaginary user sitting still at a desk.

In practice this means the app should work with whatever attention is left over. If a screen only makes sense when someone studies it, the screen is wrong.

## Working memory is small

Working memory can only hold a few things at once. Every extra option on a screen takes up some of that space, even when the person never taps it.

So I give each screen one clear decision. On Briefly's feed, the decision is "do I keep listening to this clip or move to the next one?" Everything else on the screen is quieter than that choice.

Familiar patterns help for the same reason. When a control works the way people already expect, they don't spend any memory figuring it out.

:::note A test I use
Cover the screen and ask: what is the one thing a person decides here? If I can't answer in a sentence, the screen is doing too much.
:::

## Design for the distracted, and everyone benefits

When I design for someone who is tired, distracted, or in a noisy place, the result is also better for people with visual or motor impairments. Larger touch targets, audio-first interaction, and CarPlay support all came from that idea. Accessibility was part of the first version, and it made the product better for everybody.

## Feedback builds trust

People trust a product when they can predict it. Each action should get a clear response, so the person always knows what just happened and what will happen next.

## Where to start

If you are designing something this week, try the cover-the-screen test on your busiest screen. Then remove one thing. It is usually harder than adding a feature, and it helps more.
