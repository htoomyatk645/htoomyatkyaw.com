Assalamu alaikum (peace be upon you).

Yesterday I attended the first Muslims in Business Conference at Harvard Business School with Suleiman Abuaqel. It was the largest gathering HBS has on record: thousands of Muslim entrepreneurs, students, and leaders from business and startups. I met people who want to solve modern problems while building Islamic principles and values into what they make, and it felt good to be in a room full of them.

A few things have stayed with me.

Sumaiya Balbale, one of the speakers, said that when you choose an industry or a company, you should prioritize talent density and company velocity. Surround yourself with high achievers and real expertise, at places that are growing fast or at least thinking about growth. That way you get to see the kinds of decisions made at every level of a company.

Nasser Chanda, on the panel about automation, AI and the future of work, talked about using AI as an individual, not only as a company: to multiply and complement your own skills and grow what you can contribute.

And Amjad Masad, the keynote speaker, told everyone to learn vibe coding and bring it into their businesses, whatever their size.

I'm hoping to put these lessons to work in my own ventures, in sha Allah.
